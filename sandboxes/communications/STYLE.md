# Teaching style

These rules apply to every worksheet, notebook, answer key and twin page in this repo.

They also match how I want answers in chat (`agentics/PROMPTFORMAT.md`).

## First principles

1. **Start from the bottom.** Assume nothing beyond everyday knowledge unless the learner has shown they know it. Define every technical term the first time it appears, in plain words.
2. **Concrete before abstract.** Real example, real numbers or bits first; only then the general rule. Never open with a formula.
3. **Derive or demonstrate everything.** Formulas, identities, bit tricks, protocol constants, conventions. Show why each must be true and what breaks if it weren't. Never write "just remember".
4. **Be explicit about skipped proofs.** If a full proof is out of scope: say so, give intuition, demonstrate numerically, name what is taken on trust and where it is proved later in the repo.
5. **Context on every question.** Open with 1–3 lines on where this shows up in real communications, modelling or finance work.
6. **Common misconceptions.** End each concept with a box: tempting wrong ideas and why they are wrong.
7. **Cross-link twins.** When a concept appears in both comms and quant, add an "Other lens" note linking to `/twins/`.
8. **Be accurate.** Cite standards, RFCs or textbook sections. Mark uncertain figures **VERIFY**; never invent numbers.
9. **Practise legally.** Synthetic signals or published test vectors only. Never live interception of radio traffic (UK Wireless Telegraphy Act 2006).

## Chat / tutoring extras

- One idea per short section; headings that show the build-up.
- If the learner's guess is right, say so and build on it; if wrong, show why it was tempting.
- Prefer a visual (or interactive) when seeing beats reading.
- Keep maths light; put real numbers through every formula.
- Finish by tying back to the starting question; ask which step still feels shaky.
- Stay on what was asked; mention missing important topics in one line and let the learner choose.
